The hidden problem

Most leasing failures happen before the lead ever reaches ownership reporting.

Calls go unanswered.

Web forms submit but generate no response.

Email inquiries disappear.

Follow-up stops too early.

Prospect questions go unanswered.

No one pushes clearly for the next step.

These failures usually happen before a tour is scheduled, before a real conversation happens, before the PMS shows a meaningful leasing event, and before ownership reporting flags a problem.

That means owners can spend heavily on marketing, staffing, concessions, amenities, and training while losing leases inside the execution layer.

Most owners are optimizing the front of the funnel while flying blind on whether prospects ever reach a real leasing conversation.

What surprised us

These failures appeared under normal operating conditions.

The issue was not that properties were overwhelmed by an unusual surge in demand.

Most tests were ordinary leasing interactions: routine inquiry forms, normal weekday outreach, standard phone and email channels, typical prospect questions, and basic follow-up expectations.

And still, major execution failures appeared.

Some properties never responded. Some produced automation but no human engagement. Some followed up once and stopped. Some failed to answer basic prospect questions. Some never created a clear next-step CTA.

The problem is not always demand generation. Often, the demand is already there — it just is not being worked consistently.

Financial impact

Small leasing failures create disproportionate NOI damage.

Multifamily owners already spend heavily to generate demand: marketing, ILS exposure, leasing payroll, concessions, amenities, and renovations.

But even strong traffic numbers can underperform financially if leasing execution breaks down after a prospect engages.

The economics compound quickly.

Industry-average leasing conversion rates are often around 8%. Top-performing operators can achieve 16%+.

That difference materially impacts occupancy, concession pressure, NOI, refinance leverage, and asset valuation.

Small conversion improvements can create outsized NOI gains when applied across an entire property.

Founder story

We built the visibility layer we wish we had as owners.

As a hands-on GP and operator, Scott Van Ramshorst spent years trying to understand why leasing performance varied so dramatically across properties.

Like most owners, he initially looked at pricing, marketing, concessions, staffing, training, and traffic quality.

He was on weekly calls with on-site teams, digging through reports and trying to diagnose the issue. But despite all the effort and internal reporting, he still couldn’t clearly see where the breakdown was actually happening.

The leasing results were inconsistent. Occupancy and NOI were being affected. But the operational failures causing those outcomes were largely invisible from the ownership level.

What Scott eventually discovered was simple, but critical: the biggest leasing problems were happening inside the execution layer.

Different people. Different times. Different lead sources. Different communication channels.

Some inquiries were handled well. Others were delayed, mishandled, or never meaningfully worked at all.

From the outside, it looked like a pricing problem or a marketing problem.

In reality, it was a visibility problem.

Once ownership could finally see whether leads were actually being worked consistently, the operational issues became obvious — and the improvements were significant.

Multifamily owners should not have to guess where leasing performance is breaking down.

They should be able to see it, measure it, and fix it before missed follow-up turns into missed revenue, lower NOI, and lost asset value.
